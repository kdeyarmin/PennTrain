## Corrected defects

1. Clearing a schedule's start date called date arithmetic during rendering with an invalid Date, crashing the form. Invalid/blank dates now leave the form usable and block creation until corrected; impossible calendar dates are rejected.
2. Scheduling setup kept local selections and drafts when switching facilities. Facility-keyed panels now reset selected employees, shifts, units, pending deletions and workload edits with the facility.
3. Credential warning days `0` became `90` through a truthiness fallback. Zero is now preserved; negative, fractional and non-finite windows fail before mutation. Expiration before issue date is rejected, and unavailable employee details produce an actionable message.
4. Class duration `0` or blank became one hour. Creation now requires positive finite hours while preserving fractional hours, avoiding silent training-credit changes.
5. Training-type forms accepted unusable negative/fractional renewal days and warning days. Renewal intervals require positive whole days; blank still means one-time training, and zero warning days remain valid.
6. Practicums always queried the current year, making records created for another year disappear. The page now offers a year selector and returns to the saved year. Historical learner names resolve from a lookup scoped to visible record IDs, including inactive/terminated staff; the create and observer pickers retain their active-staff filters. Name loading is explicit, and a failed name lookup preserves the underlying records with a retry. Editing an existing row locks its year to match the server's immutable identity. Year bounds and whole reminder days are validated.
7. Quiz history stopped at the API row cap, potentially hiding older passes or undercounting attempts. It now pages with a stable unique tie-break and an optional server quiz filter; failed later pages fail the entire query rather than returning partial history.
8. Credential, certificate, training-history, schedule and quiz pages could interpret failed employee-profile lookups as missing profiles or empty successful histories. They now show recoverable errors; self-service record pages distinguish a missing profile from an empty history.
9. Failed swap-request lookups hid outstanding requests. My Schedule now exposes a retry.
10. Passport loading/failure was treated as no passport, offering creation prematurely. Loading and error states are explicit; clipboard failure is caught and explained. Concurrent credential-renewal buttons are disabled while a submission is in progress.
11. Public passport QR state could outlive its slug, allowing an earlier asynchronous QR result to appear under another learner's transcript. A slug-keyed QR component clears the old image immediately, cancels obsolete results, and presents a generation-failure fallback with a verification link.
12. Employee readiness explanations displayed raw clearance codes such as `act33_child_abuse`. Known codes now use the established credential labels shared with My Credentials. Requirement matching, eligibility, and custom labels are unchanged.
13. New live classes hid their session-registration, waitlist and signed-attendance controls until the separate walk-in roster contained an attendee. The session panel is now available on empty classes. Historical walk-in attendees and session registrants resolve names through record-scoped lookups, including inactive staff, while new-registration pickers remain active-only. Name lookup failures preserve the evidence and expose retry.
14. Adding multiple class attendees rejected at the first failed request, re-enabled actions while other inserts were still pending, and left successful targets selected for retry. The bounded batch now waits for every result and retains only failures. Selected targets are checked against the current active roster before writing. Attendance, signature hashing, approval, registration and legacy roster writes coordinate their busy state so class completion cannot race a pending attendance save. Signature-generation failures retain the form and expose an error.
15. Class history and session registration queries silently stopped at the API response cap, hiding older calendar entries and large waitlists. Stable pagination now advances by the actual returned count and stops only on an empty page, including when the server cap is lower than the requested page size. Attendance evidence lookup deduplicates and batches registration IDs, with complete paging and failure propagation.
16. Retraining Monitor ignored failed facility-assignment queries and could report no available facilities instead of an access-lookup error. It now offers retry and suppresses misleading totals. Cohort enrollment preserves failed selections for retry, locks class/attendee/close controls during the batch, rechecks selected class availability, and keys the dialog by facility so later responses cannot reset a different facility's selection.
17. Expanded plan progress used the active roster as its only name source, leaving completed former staff as raw IDs. Progress now resolves names from the displayed assignments' employee IDs, keeps completed/canceled history intact, and offers retry for failed names. Plan application still uses active staff.
18. The offline reader did not create its first checkpoint until a navigation or save click, losing first-lesson reading progress and starting engagement time after that reading. It now queues the displayed first lesson once saved progress has loaded, preserves existing checkpoints, and leaves manual retry available after storage failure. A delayed failure stays visible through mutation rerenders but is suppressed after leaving the reader. This records viewed progress only; quizzes, attestations and completion still require the live course.
19. Separate IndexedDB read and write transactions let overlapping lesson saves allocate the same sequence or overwrite further progress; sync receipts could also overwrite a newer checkpoint or lower its known server version. Checkpoint updates now read and write in one transaction, resolve after commit, and merge receipt versions monotonically. A conflict replay for progress already confirmed at an equal or newer server version preserves the successful returned outcome and any newer pending action, avoiding a false review warning or unnecessary sequence rotation. Rejection and device-revocation receipts remain authoritative. Regression cases reproduce the duplicate sequence and stale-conflict behavior before their fixes and verify concurrent saves, receipts and older responses.
20. A successful shift-swap request invalidated other workforce queries but left the employee's pending-request list stale, hiding the submitted request and its withdrawal control. The mutation now refreshes that list; a real query-cache observer regression reproduces the stale empty list before the fix and observes the pending request afterward.
21. Certificate history stopped at a short page even when the API imposed a lower response limit than the requested range, hiding older awards. It now advances by actual returned rows until an empty page, retaining stable ordering and every scope filter. A later-page failure rejects the full query instead of presenting partial certificate history.
22. The quiz-history pager had the same lower-response-cap gap, undercounting previous attempts and hiding older passes. It now also advances by the actual returned count until empty. Both the older-pass omission and concealed later-page error were reproduced and covered by regressions.
23. Mobile assignment rows reserved one horizontal line for the title, status and actions, squeezing long titles into narrow columns. Rows now stack course details above wrapping actions on small screens and retain a horizontal layout on wider screens. Learning-list buttons now expose their selected state through `aria-pressed`. The observed screenshot's Required highlight beside Optional content was a transient color-transition capture: heading, filtering and button variants already share one state variable; no selection/fallback defect was found. The browser screenshot now disables animation during capture while normal product button animation is preserved.
24. Changing a single-choice or true/false answer key sent independent unmark/mark requests, so a failed or overlapping request could leave zero or multiple correct answers. The authoring UI now uses one transactional answer-selection RPC, locks related answer controls until refresh completes, and refreshes after ambiguous failures. The RPC retains caller RLS, platform-author permissions, draft-only content and question/answer membership checks. Multiple-choice editing retains its existing independent selections.
25. Clearing a new quiz's passing score silently changed its threshold to zero, and invalid question points silently became one. Shared quiz settings now preserve the existing/default threshold for blank input, retain an explicit zero, and require bounded whole-number scores, attempt limits and positive grading points. No existing passing threshold or attempt allowance is changed.
26. Credential and competency history pagers stopped at a short page, hiding additional records when the server imposed a smaller response cap. Both now advance by actual returned rows until empty; later-page errors reject the complete query. A failed or still-loading competency checklist also previously left Save available, allowing an evaluation without loaded checklist evidence. The form now blocks that submission and exposes retry without discarding the draft.
27. A partially failed bulk shift addition closed the form and discarded the failed targets. The bounded batch now waits for all requests, retains only failures for retry, reports the failed employee and reason, and locks schedule/add controls until settlement. Failed eligibility queries block submission even if stale rows remain cached; server eligibility remains authoritative.
28. Certification checklist writes, submission, decision and signature generation used separate busy states, allowing a decision while evidence was still being saved. These actions now share a busy guard through authoritative query refresh, including the asynchronous signature digest. Digest failure preserves the form for retry. Assessor permissions and qualification requirements are unchanged.
29. Course blocks, quiz questions, training-plan items and competency-template items reordered through two independent writes; a second failure could leave duplicate positions. All four now use one transactional swap RPC with a fixed resource whitelist, caller RLS, same-parent checks, expected-position checks and parent/row locking. Course content retains draft-only restrictions, and the whole swap rolls back if either row fails. Failed calls refresh the list before controls unlock.
30. Swapping items with equal positions left by an earlier partial write reported success without moving anything. A follow-up migration repairs those existing ties inside the reorder transaction: it locks the same parent's siblings, uses the readers' stable `sort_order, id` order, exchanges the selected pair and normalizes positions atomically. Other siblings retain their relative order; any failed or skipped sibling rolls back the complete repair. Non-tied swaps keep their existing path. Source inspection confirmed all four add forms already allocate `max(sort_order) + 1`; deletion gaps were not the cause.
31. Administrator qualification switched profiles without resetting uncontrolled fields and document/CE state, and autosaves sent full stale snapshots that could overwrite another field's completed save. The editor is keyed by organization/profile, waits for the initial profile read, disables writes during failed reads, and saves only the changed fields. Late results no longer toast or open signed documents after unmount. CE entries now validate positive finite hours, real dates and nonblank topics; fractional credit remains allowed. CE submission locks the draft, prevents duplicate calls and retains failed entries for retry.
32. Administrator and credential uploads deleted stored evidence after an ambiguous metadata response, even when the write might already have committed. Administrator uploads retain their path for explicit same-file metadata retry and lock the full upload/link phase. Credential uploads reconcile the exact organization, credential, bucket and path; committed metadata is recovered, uncertain bytes remain, and cleanup requires a definite database rejection plus a confirmed absent row.
33. Partial course-assignment batches closed the enrollment form and discarded failed employee selections; partial completion batches similarly discarded failed assignment selections. Both now wait for every bounded request and retain only failed targets for retry. Enrollment controls and close actions stay locked during submission; loading/failed employee or version queries cannot submit stale selections.
34. Governed-content registration and snapshot creation offered no retry for failed source queries and could act on stale cached selections. They now expose retries and require current eligible sources. Revision drafts reset with the selected asset, and authoring/review controls stay locked while their request is pending so completion cannot discard a newly edited draft.
35. A failed standards-package completion lookup left the learner's course step blocked with no recovery control. The player now exposes an inline retry while preserving its active iframe and progress. Package completion and course completion remain distinct server-controlled events.
36. Class roster replacement reused a filename with overwrite enabled and deleted bytes/metadata after an uncertain class-link response. It now uses the shared reconciled uploader with unique paths, preserving previous roster evidence. An uncertain link retains the new document and offers retry using the same document ID; class completion waits for that link to resolve. Upload/link controls share a synchronous busy guard, and late results finish only the captured class operation without feedback in another view.

37. My Schedule allowed duplicate in-flight commands, edits or dialog dismissal during pending requests, and feedback after leaving the page. Time-off, shift-swap, withdrawal and open-shift claims now share a synchronous guard and pending controls, preserve failed drafts, and suppress feedback after unmount. A failed candidate lookup exposes retry and cannot submit a stale cached shift. Server eligibility and approval rules are unchanged.
38. Assignment exemptions defaulted to the current year even when the roster displayed another year, and reused the previous year or employee's reason. The editor now follows the roster year, resets with employee/year identity and reloads the authoritative reason when reopened. Existing required assignments still count; no qualification or regulatory exemption was added.
39. Completed-class descriptive and attendance corrections could run independently while either audit mutation was pending. They now share one synchronous guard, lock both correction drafts until settlement, retain rejected input, and suppress late success/error feedback after leaving the class. The server remains responsible for immutable evidence and correction audit records.
40. Bulk plan-course selection retained IDs that a same-plan refetch had removed from the eligible picker, causing the entire insert to fail on an already-added course that the user could no longer deselect. Submission and counts now use the current eligible selection, explain excluded choices and refuse an empty batch or failed/unready prerequisite reads. Courses hidden only by search remain selected. New-item order continues from the highest existing position, and required/optional choices are preserved.
41. Credential-renewal approval left metadata, reasons and dismissal available while the decision was in flight, and emitted late feedback after unmount. The dialog now locks the captured decision, blocks duplicate submissions and dismissal until settlement, retains rejected fields for retry and suppresses obsolete feedback. Independent-review, scan-state and database authorization requirements are unchanged.

42. HRIS staged rows and source choices stopped at the API row cap, and the run picker exposed only the newest twenty runs, leaving older blocked imports unreachable. All three reads now retain explicit organization/run scope, advance by actual returned rows to an empty page, use stable ID tie-breaks and honor cancellation. Later-page failures reject the collection instead of concealing undecided rows.
43. HRIS source, run and row-decision forms allowed pending draft changes, and old run results could close a newer row editor or select a run after leaving its organization. Source and run workspaces now reset by organization/run, commands use synchronous guards and pending controls, and obsolete callbacks suppress feedback. The captured start request preserves its idempotency key on failure. Decisions and uncertain source registrations refresh their authoritative collections before unlocking; changed/failed row lookups cannot submit stale link candidates.
44. HRIS Apply discarded its `{applied, skipped, failed}` response and always announced completion, even after per-row failures. The run-specific controls now show actual batch counts and explicit failed-row recovery guidance, serialize validation/application with row decisions, and require an eligible selected run and current rows. Only pending rows can be applied; failed rows require corrected source data staged in a new run rather than a misleading retry of already-processed rows.
45. HRIS duplicate-candidate choices showed only shortened UUIDs, preventing a meaningful human comparison. They now use the existing authorized employee-by-ID lookup for server-supplied candidates only and display names, staff numbers and email before decision and in the picker. Lookup errors offer retry, pending/failed lookups block decisions, and unavailable identities are labelled explicitly and cannot be linked. Peer review also found that the lookup's 200-ID URL chunks could themselves exceed a lower server row cap: each chunk now independently pages by actual count until empty, with stable ordering and cancellation, and fails the whole read on a later error. No candidate authority or employee permission was broadened.

46. The manager self-service queue allowed dismissal or replacement of a pending approval draft, so an earlier time-off, claim or swap response could clear the newer decision. It now serializes all three decision kinds, locks scope/reason/close controls until settlement, retains failed reasons, suppresses callbacks after unmount and rechecks current queue membership and swap expiration at submission. Expired swaps remain rejectable. Failed queue/facility reads expose retries and block stale decisions. A failed mutation response now awaits an authoritative queue refresh before releasing pending controls, preventing a committed decision with a lost acknowledgement from remaining silently retryable; failed refreshes preserve the draft and block submission until successful read recovery.
47. The same queue ignored the platform administrator's Viewed organization and stopped each pending-request collection at the API cap. Its organization-keyed workspace now scopes all three reads explicitly, validates the selected facility and clearly labels the intentional all-organizations view when no organization is selected. Complete paging retains filters and unique ordering on every page, propagates cancellation and rejects later-page errors instead of showing partial counts. The shared facility-choice reader had the same truncation gap, which could falsely reject a current facility absent from its first page; it now also reads through an empty page, preserving organization scope, enabled behavior, stable name/ID order and cancellation.
