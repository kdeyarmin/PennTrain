# Release readiness plan

> **Not the backlog.** See [BACKLOG.md](../../BACKLOG.md) for open work; every finding below is a
> Tier J row there, and that row is where status lives. This document is the plan for taking
> CareMetric CareBase from "pilot-ready in code" to a release a real facility can run on: what was
> verified, what a full read of the application found wrong or self-contradictory, the order to
> fix it in, and the gate that says "ready". It is the third readiness pass; it builds on
> [GO_LIVE_READINESS_REVIEW_PLAN.md](GO_LIVE_READINESS_REVIEW_PLAN.md) (the general-availability
> bar) and [PILOT_READINESS_PLAN.md](PILOT_READINESS_PLAN.md) (the pilot bar, the onboarding
> runbook and the daily checks), neither of which is repeated here.

**Prepared:** 2026-09-06 (afternoon UTC), against `main` @ `41c2968` (PR #492, merged 13:44 UTC and
deployed by run 183 at 13:55 UTC) and the production project `xsqobvvreaovwibxwyvv`. Everything in
section 3 was observed after that deploy.

**How this pass differed from the previous two.** The 2026-09-04 passes reviewed the operational
layer, then read eight areas for functional defects. This pass asked a narrower question -- *is
the application ready to release, and what in it is wrong or does not make logical sense* -- and
answered it three ways: every automated gate was run here (including the database lane on a clean
stack), production was probed read-only after the latest deploy, and twelve independent reviewers
each read one area of the application end to end with the same brief (identity and shell; Train;
workforce and scheduling; resident care, clinical and offline; safety, incidents and survey;
policy, documents, regulatory and help; platform, billing and notifications; imports, exports and
jobs; the September migrations and the exclusion-screening removal; the public and guest surfaces
and the server; cross-cutting frontend patterns; the voice gateway and integrations). Every
finding they returned that is called "verified" below was re-read against the source by the lead
reviewer, and the one that mattered most was confirmed by a read-only probe of production. Rows the
earlier passes marked `done` were re-checked on this branch; where a row's status is wrong, section
4.4 says so.

## 1. Verdict

> **Superseded in part by section 0.** Everything below is the verdict as it stood before the fix
> pass, and it is left standing because it is the argument for what was done. The code half of it
> is now closed; what remains is console work, two owner decisions, and observing any of it on
> production.

**Not ready to release today. Ready for the first facility in roughly four weeks of single-threaded
work if section 6 is followed in order, with one tenant-boundary defect and two regressions that shipped in
the last 48 hours fixed first.**

The base is strong and every gate is green: 654 migrations replay from empty, 3,865 pgTAP
assertions, 1,754 unit tests, 273 edge-function tests and sixteen static gates pass here and in CI
on this exact commit; production carries the same 654 migrations byte for byte, 72 of 72 edge
functions, zero advisor errors and zero grant drift; every critical job is green except the one
that is waiting on a Stripe secret. What this pass found is not configuration. It is that the
application, read end to end, has 21 defects (one of them a tenant-boundary hole) a facility would hit in its first weeks with no
workaround, 59 more that are wrong with a workaround, and 70 that are recorded so nobody
rediscovers them. 18 earlier rows carry a status or a claim this pass found wrong or incomplete.

The six that decide the verdict:

- **A tenant can write its own entitlement input.** `organizations_update` admits an org_admin and the
  column guard covers four columns, so one direct write extends the tenant's own trial for ever, and
  another flips it to `is_demo`, which switches off mandatory MFA, passes the AI BAA gate and starts the
  nightly demo re-seed inserting fixtures into a real tenant. The pgTAP suite proves the write path is
  live. One trigger change closes it.

- **Two regressions from the 2026-09-05 fixes are live on production.** Every manager edit of a
  shift assignment fails with `permission denied for schema app_private` (the swap-approval fix
  made a trigger call a helper the browser role cannot reach; probed on production: the role holds
  neither USAGE nor EXECUTE), and every credential-renewal review click raises "not ready for a
  human decision" because the malware-verdict fix changed what the worker writes but not what the
  review RPC accepts. Both are one-migration fixes; neither has a test that runs as the browser
  role, which is why the suites are green.
- **Four state machines dead-end.** A learner who fails a seeded final assessment three times is
  stuck with no product path out, and so is that annual requirement; an incident with any
  completed corrective action can never be approved or closed because the column both engines
  demand has no writer; rehire and return-from-leave cannot be started from any screen; and the
  AAL2 step-up wall covers far more of the product than the register records, including every
  demo organization, which the login gate exempts and the operation gate does not.
- **Regulatory clocks are anchored on the wrong dates.** Assessment completion is stamped with the
  upload day and the next annual cycle computed from it; every bulk-imported resident is admitted
  on the import day and gets a whole compliance timeline from it; a plan of correction is "overdue"
  in the work queue the evening before its deadline and "overdue" in the escalation job the day
  after.
- **Records that read as current are not.** Publishing a new policy version leaves every
  old-version attestation counted as current and still signable; declarative attestation campaigns
  enrol new hires into every past cycle forever; discharging a resident from their page never
  releases the bed; activating a revised support plan leaves the old plan's tasks on the Floor for
  two weeks.
- **The public site sells a customer-operated course builder and AI course creation** that only
  the platform team has -- deliberate copy, pinned by a test, contradicting the product.

None of this is a rewrite. Every P1 has a fix sized S or M in section 4.1, and most of them are
one migration or one component. The operational blockers from the earlier passes are unchanged
and still need a person with a console: MFA enrolment (zero verified factors across the five
privileged accounts, and it is now the unblock for a dozen product flows), the provider secrets
(zero notification deliveries in the life of the project, zero sign-ins in the last thirty days),
the backup restore drill, and the Auth dashboard toggles.

## 2. What "ready to release" means

The pilot bar in [PILOT_READINESS_PLAN.md](PILOT_READINESS_PLAN.md) section 2 (P1-P10) stands and
is not repeated. This pass sharpens it with what a *release* adds to a pilot:

| # | Requirement | Why it cannot slip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | No P1 row in section 4.1 is open, and each is closed with the test that would have caught it, run as the role that hits it | Two of them shipped on 2026-09-05 through a green suite that runs as superuser |
| R2 | Every regulatory clock the product computes (assessment cycles, admission timelines, incident deadlines, POC deadlines, drill cadence) is anchored on a date a person entered or the regulation names, never on "when the file was uploaded" or "when the row was imported" | A wrong date on a surveyor-facing record is worse than no date |
| R3 | Every state machine has a path out of every state it can enter (assignment, incident, employee lifecycle, class, campaign, resident) | A dead end in week one is a support call the owner has to answer with SQL |
| R4 | What the public site promises is what the product does for the role that buys it | The course-builder claim is the headline of the Train plan |
| R5 | The AAL2 step-up is enrolled for the privileged accounts, warns before the call, and is off where the product says it is off (demo organizations) | Until then a manager's day ends in raw `42501` toasts |
| R6 | The earlier rows this pass found mis-stated (section 4.4) carry corrected status | The register is enforced; a `done` that is not done is how it drifted before |
| R7 | The pilot plan's operational gates (MFA, providers, restore drill, Auth toggles, the BAA) are met | Unchanged from 2026-09-04 and still the long pole |

**Deliberately not required for the release** (record the decision, then move on): live Stripe
(comp the first organizations; H9 stays for general availability), real SCORM vendor packages,
SG-2 activation, multi-state rule packs, the unclassified-table sweep for pillar packages nobody
has bought yet (J-rows on modules, until an Essentials/Workforce contract is sold), the voice
gateway's JWT persistence if the gateway is not deployed for the pilot, and the P3 long tail.

  the facility day; `WorkItemDetail` shows one due instant two ways. Every compliance-deciding path

  on it.
