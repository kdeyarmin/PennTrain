I'm Kevin, and I want to talk about the training topic people are most tempted to click through, and the one I've watched change outcomes the most.

I've spent over twenty years in senior care. Five of them running a nursing home, the last seventeen in hospice. I've walked into more personal care homes and assisted living facilities than I can count, usually because somebody called me when something needed fixing. And the thing I've seen quietly take more residents than almost anything else is infection.

Here's why it matters more in your building than almost anywhere else. The people you care for live together, eat together, and share staff, equipment, and air. Many of them have immune systems that don't respond the way yours does. A respiratory infection that puts you on the couch for two days can put one of your residents in the hospital, and sometimes they don't come back the same person they were when they left.

So let's start with the one that outperforms everything else, and it isn't close. Hand hygiene.

Before and after resident contact. Before you handle food or help with medication. After anything soiled. After gloves come off, every single time. Gloves are not a substitute for washing your hands, because hands get contaminated when gloves come off, and gloves have small defects you will never see.

And know when soap and water is not optional. If a resident has diarrhea, or you're told a resident has C. diff, alcohol rub will not do the job. Those spores survive it. You need soap, water, and the friction of actually scrubbing, because what removes them is the physical washing and the rinse.

The second habit is protective equipment used the way your facility trains you to use it, and the part people get wrong is not putting it on. It's taking it off. You remove the dirty items in a sequence that keeps the outside of them away from your skin and your face, and you clean your hands in between. If nobody has ever walked you through your facility's exact sequence, that's a fair thing to ask for, and you should ask for it this week rather than the week after something happens.

I'll be honest with you about why this course exists as its own hour instead of a paragraph in a handbook. Every one of these habits is easy on a calm day and hard on a short-staffed one, and the short-staffed day is exactly when it matters. Nobody skips handwashing because they don't believe in it. They skip it because they're behind. That's the whole problem, and knowing that is most of the solution.
